# Calendar film v3 — let the result create the question

Target: **66 seconds**, approximately 120 spoken words. One uninterrupted macro film, with an optional April/May replay afterward. The viewer should understand one causal chain: a tooth meets the extra driving finger, the ring takes an extra step; a gap meets it, the extra step does not happen.

Open on the recognizable reference 326939, rather than an unexplained collection of movement parts. Let the dial perform the apparently impossible change first. Its mechanism then becomes the answer to something the viewer has just seen.

## Why the rolling-wheel explanation can be understood

Give the viewer three visual anchors: the fixed central wheel, a dot on the satellite, and the inspection location. Keep the camera’s orientation stable while demonstrating the orbit. A rotating camera would conceal the very orientation change being taught.

The satellite is attached to the date ring by its pivot, but free to turn on that pivot. Its meshing teeth must remain in contact with the fixed wheel as the ring carries it around. The viewer sees why it turns; no equation or ratio is needed in the film. When the pivot returns to the inspection location, the marked wheel faces differently. The extra finger now sees empty space instead of a tooth.

Use the continuous angles from `calendar-kinematics.ts`. **April 30→May 1 advances two of the 31 ring pitches.** Its satellite centre must therefore end roughly 23.23° beyond the inspection point. **May 1→May 30 adds 29 more pitches**. Together those phases complete one full 31-position ring orbit from April 30 to May 30. Resetting the satellite to the inspection point on May 1 would break both the mechanics and the explanation.

The four program fingers occupy a small sector in the derived explanatory configuration. In April, a finger in this sector faces the contact ray. By May, the sector has turned well away. That conspicuous change is the visual argument. A faint before/after pose is useful; a persistent diagram, arc, numeric angle or orbit trail is unnecessary.

## Staging rules

- **One new part at a time.** A body may remain visible when its next feature is revealed. Do not spawn the date ring, both driving fingers, cam, spring and all satellite gears simultaneously.
- **One active contact at a time.** Warm light should travel from the driving finger, through the contacted tooth, into the moving ring. The fixed central wheel is the visible constraint when rolling is explained. Avoid decorative pulses between unrelated gears.
- **Let contact explain force.** Show approach, touch, ring movement, then separation. During May’s empty sweep, the ring must remain stationary until the ordinary finger engages.
- **Use the actual drive order.** In the slowed April jump, correction is first and ordinary drive follows, within one release. Do not show an ordinary step followed by a second independent correction.
- **Keep the spring/cam lesson for a later close-up.** This film can truthfully say stored energy is released while keeping those parts outside its teaching frame. Introducing another mechanism during the crucial tooth/gap comparison would dilute the explanation.
- **Use one brief caption at most.** Candidate moments: “31 positions”, “Extra step”, “Daily step”, and “Same place. Different orientation.” Most frames need no caption.
- **Keep the desired object present.** Begin and end on the complete watch; retain believable metallic scale, deep reflections, shallow depth of field and the recognizable date-window context during the mechanism shots. Avoid making the main experience resemble a classroom dashboard.
- **Use sound to join the phases.** A winding tension and one released gesture support the jump. The two slowed contacts should not sound like two separate midnights. Let May’s missed engagement have a small silence.
- **Give the voice space.** Warm, low-register feminine narration can be intimate and assured. Prioritize clean articulation of “thirty”, “thirty-one” and “first”; forced breathiness would obscure the essential comparison.

Afterward, the compact interaction can be **April / May / Replay**. Selecting a month should establish its phase and return to date 30 before running the same contact shot. Twelve month buttons and ratio labels are not needed for the first understanding. A separate deeper view may expose the full-year behavior and sources.

## Evidence boundary

**Confirmed production behavior for the original 2012 calibre 9001:** the fixed central wheel and orbiting satellite architecture; four correction fingers; automatic 30-day corrections in April, June, September and November; a double date advance within milliseconds; and an annual correction on 1 March. Source: [Rolex’s 2012 launch dossier, PDF page 6](https://www.thewatch.co.il/image/users/190146/ftp/my_files/ROLEX/Sky-Dweller_EN.pdf). The same dossier identifies calibre 9001 and reference 326939 on pages 10 and 12.

**Documented patent mechanism used to make the physical explanation concrete:** a 31-position ring; two driving fingers; the correction finger preceding the ordinary finger; 30→31 followed by 31→1 in one cam/spring release. Source: [Rolex patent EP1596261B1, description 0020–0028](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP1596261B1/en). This is a patent example, not authenticated production CAD.

**Illustrative configuration:** the demonstrator combines the patent’s 123/36 rolling ratio with four program fingers. That combination is mathematically consistent with the four production corrections and is tested, but its counts, local finger slots and exact assembled phase have not been established for the production 9001. The later [Rolex patent application US20190179264A1, paragraphs 0057–0059](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20190179264A1/en) explicitly documents a four-tooth variant excluding February; it does not authenticate the demonstrator’s exact geometry as a manufactured 9001.

**Editorial choices:** the 66-second pacing, equal slow-motion halves, tracking dot, selected warm highlights, transparent/receding dial, before/after overlay and expanded audible contacts. The exact production tooth flanks, centre distances, driver sweep, spring force, cam contour and contact durations remain unverified. The film must not claim to be a measured CAD reconstruction or show the illustrative tooth counts as confirmed Rolex production specifications. Put this distinction in the deeper source panel rather than overloading the opening film.

The explanation should never imply that the satellite knows the civil date electronically, that April alone receives a correction, that February is automatically solved, or that a 30-day month reduces the ring to 30 physical positions.
